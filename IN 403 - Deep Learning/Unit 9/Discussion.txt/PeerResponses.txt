Hi Brittany,

Great choice focusing on PHI/data exfiltration — your point about disguised requests mimicking legitimate sources is an important real-world concern. One gap I noticed in your plan is that while you mention PHI detection and redaction as a technical guardrail, you do not address the retrieval layer itself. If the chatbot's underlying knowledge base already contains PHI-laden documents, detection at the output stage becomes the last line of defense rather than one layer among several. A pre-indexing data curation step — where every document is screened and sanitized before it enters the retrieval corpus — would prevent PHI from ever reaching the generation pipeline in the first place, significantly reducing the surface area for leakage (Yao et al., 2024).

One additional control that could reduce false ALLOWs is implementing a secondary classifier that reviews the chatbot's drafted response before it is displayed to the user. This two-stage check — first at retrieval, then at generation — catches cases where the NER filter misses PHI that is embedded in abbreviations, misspellings, or coded language that staff may use informally (e.g., referring to a patient by room number or initials).

Here is a question that might shift your recommendation: If internal testing revealed that the PHI detection system had a false-negative rate above 5% — meaning one in twenty PHI-containing responses slipped through undetected — would you still recommend deploying with conditions, or would you pause deployment until that rate dropped below an acceptable threshold?
