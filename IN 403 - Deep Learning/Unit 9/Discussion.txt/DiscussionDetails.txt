The Policy Chatbot Is Being Attacked — What Do You Do?

A hospital launches a policy chatbot to help staff find answers quickly. Within the first week, several issues occur:
1. Users try to trick it into revealing internal documents (prompt injection)
2. Someone asks for patient details (PHI/data exfiltration)
3. A few prompts request unsafe medical advice
4. The chatbot generates an inappropriate response during a stressful shift

Leadership asks for an immediate plan to reduce risk while keeping the chatbot useful.

Initial Post (250–350 words)

Pick one attack category to focus on: prompt injection, PHI/data exfiltration, jailbreaks, or unsafe medical advice. Then address the following:
1. Attack overview. Briefly describe what the attack looks like in practice and why it is likely to occur in a hospital setting.
2. Risk level. Explain why this attack is high-risk. Include one potential impact (patient safety, legal/compliance, operational disruption) and one reason it is plausible or likely.
3. Controls and safeguards.Propose three controls to reduce risk, including:
3.1. One technical guardrail (for example: policy-only answering, refusal rules, retrieval constraints, PHI detection/redaction)
3.2. One monitoring or logging control (for example: alerting, audit logs, weekly review of blocked prompts)
3.3. One governance or process control (for example: access restrictions, staff training, incident response workflow)
4. Escalation policy. Explain when the assistant should refuse, when it should escalate to a human, and who should be notified.
5. End your post with a clear recommendation: deploy, deploy with conditions, or pause deployment.
