Your Hospital AI Is Failing—Who Owns the Risk? — Initial Post

System Chosen: Skin Cancer Triage Support

1. Failure Modes

I chose the skin cancer triage support system because it operates at the intersection of clinical decision-making and patient safety — where failures have the most direct human impact. After six months in production, I believe the most likely failure modes for this system are:

Concept drift and fairness degradation. The model was trained on a specific distribution of dermatological images, but the patient population presenting for triage may shift over time — new demographics, seasonal skin conditions, or referral pattern changes. More critically, skin cancer detection models are well-documented to perform worse on darker skin tones due to training data imbalances (Daneshjou et al., 2022). If the hospital serves a diverse population but the model was trained predominantly on lighter-skinned patients, its miss rate (false negatives) for melanoma in darker-skinned patients could be significantly higher — a fairness failure with life-threatening consequences.

False negatives (missed cases). For a triage system, a false negative means a potentially cancerous lesion is classified as low-priority and the patient waits longer for specialist review — or worse, is reassured and sent home. Staff reports of "missed cases" suggest this is already happening.

Calibration drift. Even if the model's ranking ability (AUC) remains stable, its confidence scores may have drifted out of calibration. A system that says "30% chance of malignancy" when the true rate for that cohort is 60% will cause clinicians to under-prioritize genuinely urgent cases.

2. Monitoring Plan: 3 Metrics

I propose the following three metrics for ongoing monitoring:

Metric 1 (Drift): Population Stability Index (PSI) on input features — measured weekly. I would track PSI on the distribution of image characteristics (lesion size, color variance, patient age, body location) between the training reference set and the current month's inputs. A PSI > 0.2 on any feature triggers an investigation. This catches data drift before it impacts predictions.

Metric 2 (Outcome): False negative rate (sensitivity) stratified by skin tone — measured monthly. I would track the percentage of biopsy-confirmed malignancies that the model initially classified as low-priority. Critically, this metric must be stratified by Fitzpatrick skin type to detect fairness degradation. An overall sensitivity below 90% or a subgroup gap exceeding 10 percentage points triggers escalation.

Metric 3 (Operational): Clinician override rate — measured weekly. I would track how often clinicians override the model's triage recommendation (either upgrading a low-priority case or downgrading a high-priority one). A rising override rate signals that clinicians are losing trust in the system because its recommendations no longer match their clinical judgment — an early behavioral indicator of model degradation that often precedes formal performance metrics.

3. Governance Action: Continue with Conditions

I recommend continuing the system with conditions rather than pausing or retiring it. My reasoning:

- The system provides genuine clinical value — faster triage means earlier specialist review for high-risk lesions, which directly improves melanoma survival rates.
- However, the reported "missed cases" indicate the system cannot operate in its current state without additional safeguards.
- Conditions: (1) All model "low-priority" classifications for patients with Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI must receive mandatory secondary review within 48 hours until the fairness gap is confirmed below 10%. (2) The model must be retrained on a more representative dataset within 90 days. (3) Weekly PSI monitoring must be operational within 30 days.

4. Accountability and Escalation

Accountable roles:
- Model Owner: Chief of Dermatology — accountable for clinical accuracy and appropriate use
- Technical Owner: ML Engineering Lead — accountable for model performance, drift monitoring, and retraining
- Governance Authority: AI Governance Committee — accountable for approve/pause/retire decisions
- Data Owner: Health Informatics Director — accountable for training data quality and representativeness

Notification requirements:
- Any false negative confirmed by biopsy → notify Model Owner (Dermatology Chief) within 24 hours
- PSI threshold exceeded → notify Technical Owner (ML Engineering) immediately
- Fairness gap exceeds 10% in any subgroup → notify AI Governance Committee within 48 hours
- Any patient harm attributed to model recommendation → notify CMO, Risk Management, and Legal within 4 hours

Escalation path:
1. Technical Owner detects issue → investigates within 48 hours
2. If issue confirmed → notifies Model Owner + AI Governance Committee
3. Governance Committee convenes within 5 business days → decides: continue monitoring, impose new conditions, or pause
4. If patient harm occurs → immediate pause, CMO notification, incident report filed, root cause analysis within 10 days
