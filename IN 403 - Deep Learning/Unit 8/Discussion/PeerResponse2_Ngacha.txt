Response to Kevin Ngacha

Kevin, I found your post especially interesting because we chose the same system — a RAG-based hospital policy assistant — which gives me a good basis for comparison. Your point about RAG reducing hallucinations by grounding responses in approved policy documents is well taken and consistent with what Lewis et al. (2020) demonstrated for knowledge-intensive tasks.

One assumption I think could weaken your defense is the treatment of retrieval grounding as a near-complete solution to the hallucination problem. You noted that "RAG significantly reduces hallucinations," which is true, but the generation side of the pipeline can still fail even when retrieval works correctly. The LLM can misinterpret a retrieved passage, selectively quote from it in a misleading way, or incorrectly synthesize information across multiple retrieved documents. In other words, retrieving the right document does not guarantee that the generated answer accurately reflects what that document says. A compliance officer could challenge the system on exactly this point — that retrieval grounding alone is not sufficient without a mechanism to verify the quality of the generation step.

As an additional safeguard, I would suggest implementing a retrieval confidence threshold with a refusal mechanism. If no document in the corpus meets a minimum cosine similarity score — for example, 0.25 — the system should refuse to generate an answer entirely and instead direct the user to contact the appropriate department. This is something I incorporated into my own system design, drawing from the Unit 5 discussion on how ungrounded LLM responses represent the highest-risk failure mode in healthcare. A hard threshold ensures the model either answers from strong evidence or does not answer at all, rather than attempting to generate from weakly relevant material.

One question that could significantly change your recommendation: if the hospital's policy corpus was found to contain contradictory guidance across departments — for example, two active policies with conflicting PPE requirements for the same scenario — how would the system handle that conflict? If the RAG pipeline retrieves both documents and the LLM generates an answer that follows one policy while ignoring the other, staff could unknowingly violate an active policy. Would discovering widespread contradictions in the document corpus change your recommendation from "deploy with conditions" to "delay deployment" until a full policy reconciliation is completed?

Reference

Lewis, P., Perez, E., Piktus, A., Petroni, F., Karpukhin, V., Goyal, N., Kuttler, H., Lewis, M., Yih, W., Rocktaschel, T., Riedel, S., & Kiela, D. (2020). Retrieval-augmented generation for knowledge-intensive NLP tasks. Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems, 33, 9459-9474.
