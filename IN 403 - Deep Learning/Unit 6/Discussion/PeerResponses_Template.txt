One missing risk I identified: [adapt based on their post — examples]:
- If they focused on technical monitoring only: They did not address the organizational response gap — who receives the alert when drift is detected? If the alert goes to an IT team that does not understand the clinical implications, it could sit unaddressed for weeks. Monitoring without a clear escalation pathway is just observation, not protection.
- If they proposed frequent retraining: The risk of training on corrupted or biased data. If drift was caused by a data collection error (e.g., a system bug marking patients as no-shows when the appointment was actually rescheduled), retraining would permanently encode that error into the model.
- If they discussed PPE detection: The risk of adversarial adaptation — if staff learn where cameras are positioned, they might comply in monitored areas but not in unmonitored ones, giving the model artificially good performance scores that mask real non-compliance.

One improvement I would suggest: [adapt]:
- Add a "drift budget" concept — define in advance how much drift the hospital is willing to tolerate before action is required. This prevents two failure modes: over-reacting to normal variation (wasting resources) and under-reacting to serious drift (accepting degraded performance).
- Implement A/B comparison when retraining: run the old and new model side-by-side on the same patients for one week, compare predictions, and only switch to the new model if it demonstrably outperforms on recent outcomes.

"What if" question: [adapt]:
- What if two types of drift occur simultaneously (e.g., both the input data AND the underlying patient behavior change at the same time)? Your detection method catches one — would it catch the other?
- What if the hospital cannot afford to take the model offline because scheduling depends on it entirely? Is there a graceful degradation strategy that maintains some value while reducing risk?
- What if staff stop trusting the model after one publicized failure — how would you rebuild confidence even after fixing the technical issue?
