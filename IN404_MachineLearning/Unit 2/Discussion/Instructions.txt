Professor Instructions:
Before selecting a machine learning algorithm, it is important to understand the data being used. Structured data is organized in a defined format, such as customer records, sales transactions, or patient databases. Unstructured data does not follow a predefined structure and includes text, images, audio, and video. For example, machine learning can analyze customer reviews for sentiment or medical images for disease detection.

Data can also be qualitative or quantitative. Qualitative data describes categories or characteristics, such as customer satisfaction levels or product types. Quantitative data represents measurable values, such as income, temperature, age, or number of purchases. Understanding these differences helps determine how data should be prepared and which machine learning techniques may be appropriate.

The level of measurement is also important. Nominal data represents categories without an order, while ordinal data has an order but unequal differences between categories. Interval data has equal intervals but no true zero, while ratio data has equal intervals and a meaningful zero. These distinctions can influence how features are encoded and whether classification, regression, clustering, or other machine learning methods are appropriate.
