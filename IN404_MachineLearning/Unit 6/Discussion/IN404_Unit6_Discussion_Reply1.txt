To build on your discussion of the backend layer, I did some additional research and found that Matplotlib actually supports two distinct types of backends: interactive and non-interactive. Interactive backends like Qt5Agg, TkAgg, and macOS Cocoa render figures in a live GUI window where users can pan, zoom, and resize, while non-interactive (or "hardcopy") backends like Agg, PDF, and SVG write static images directly to files without any display window. The "Agg" that appears in many backend names stands for the Anti-Grain Geometry library, which is a high-performance rendering engine that Matplotlib uses for producing clean, anti-aliased raster output (The Matplotlib Development Team, 2024). I thought this was interesting because it explains why PNG output from Matplotlib always looks so crisp compared to some other plotting tools.

I also appreciated your point about pyplot being useful for quickly visualizing new datasets. In my experience with this unit's code, I noticed that our file actually uses a mix of pyplot commands (like plt.show()) and the object-oriented style (like ax.set_xlabel()). It seems like combining both gives us the convenience of pyplot for simple operations while still having precise control over individual plot elements when needed.
